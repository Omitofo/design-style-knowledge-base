# Jakob Nielsen — 10 Usability Heuristics

These heuristics remain one of the most practical, widely taught checklists for interface evaluation. They are rules of thumb, not rigid laws.

1. **Visibility of system status**  
   The system should always keep users informed about what is going on, through appropriate feedback within a reasonable time.

2. **Match between system and the real world**  
   Speak the users’ language, follow real-world conventions, make information appear in a natural and logical order.

3. **User control and freedom**  
   Support undo and redo. Provide clear "emergency exits".

4. **Consistency and standards**  
   Users should not have to wonder whether different words, situations, or actions mean the same thing. Follow platform and industry conventions.

5. **Error prevention**  
   Even better than good error messages is a careful design that prevents problems from occurring in the first place.

6. **Recognition rather than recall**  
   Minimize the user’s memory load by making objects, actions, and options visible.

7. **Flexibility and efficiency of use**  
   Accelerators — unseen by the novice user — may often speed up the interaction for the expert user.

8. **Aesthetic and minimalist design**  
   Dialogues should not contain information that is irrelevant or rarely needed. Every extra unit of information competes with relevant units.

9. **Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors**  
   Error messages should be expressed in plain language, precisely indicate the problem, and constructively suggest a solution.

10. **Help and documentation**  
   Even though it is better if the system can be used without documentation, it may be necessary to provide help. Help should be searchable, focused on the user’s task, and concrete.

## Application Note
These heuristics pair extremely well with Norman’s concepts and with visual systems that prioritize clarity (Swiss, Minimalism). Heuristic #8 explicitly supports minimalist visual approaches when they serve the task.

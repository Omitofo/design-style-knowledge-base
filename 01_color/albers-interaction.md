# Josef Albers — Interaction of Color (Key Lessons)

Primary source: *Interaction of Color* (Yale University Press). Albers taught at the Bauhaus, Black Mountain College, and Yale. The book is a practical and philosophical investigation into how colors behave in relation to one another.

## Central Thesis
Color is the most relative medium in art. The same physical color can appear completely different depending on its context. We almost never see a color as it "is"; we see it as it interacts.

## Practical Lessons for Designers

1. **Context changes everything**  
   A color’s perceived hue, value, and intensity shift dramatically based on surrounding colors. Always evaluate palettes in the actual composition, not in isolation.

2. **Ground and figure relationships**  
   The same color can advance or recede, feel warm or cool, depending on what it sits against.

3. **Quantity and placement matter**  
   A small accent of a strong color behaves differently from a large field of the same color.

4. **Value (lightness) is often more important than hue**  
   Strong value contrast drives hierarchy and legibility more reliably than hue contrast alone.

5. **Exercise-based learning**  
   Albers’s method is experimental: place colors next to each other, observe, adjust. Design systems should be tested in real layouts, not only in abstract swatches.

## Application Rules Derived from Albers
- Never finalize a palette from a style guide alone — test it on real content and backgrounds.
- When creating dark mode or alternative themes, re-evaluate every interaction, not just invert.
- Use interaction deliberately: a muted color can appear vivid next to a duller neighbor.
- For accessibility, prioritize value contrast; hue contrast alone is insufficient for many users.

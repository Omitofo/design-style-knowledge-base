# Gestalt Principles in Design

Gestalt psychology describes how humans perceive whole forms rather than isolated parts. These principles are foundational for layout, interface design, and visual hierarchy.

## Core Principles

### Proximity
Elements that are close together are perceived as a group. Use spacing to create meaningful clusters and separate unrelated items.

### Similarity
Elements that share visual characteristics (color, shape, size, orientation) are perceived as related. Use consistency to signal category membership.

### Continuity
The eye prefers continuous paths and lines. Align elements to create implied lines and flow.

### Closure
The mind completes incomplete shapes. Useful for logos and icons; also explains why partial borders or frames can still feel complete.

### Figure-Ground
We distinguish objects (figure) from their background (ground). Ambiguous figure-ground relationships can be used deliberately or can cause confusion.

### Common Fate
Elements that move or change together are perceived as a group (especially relevant in motion and interaction design).

### Prägnanz (Good Form)
We tend to perceive the simplest, most stable form possible.

## Practical Application
- Use proximity and similarity as primary grouping tools before adding lines or boxes.
- Align to create continuity and reduce visual noise.
- Test figure-ground contrast for legibility and focus.
- In interfaces, consistent treatment of interactive elements leverages similarity and common fate.

## Related
- Directly supports Swiss grid thinking and Norman’s signifiers.
- Essential for both minimal and maximal compositions — the principles still operate when density is high.

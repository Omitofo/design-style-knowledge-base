# Braun — Design Language Lessons (Rams Era and Legacy)

## Overall Positioning
Braun, particularly under Dieter Rams and his team (1950s–1980s), produced some of the purest examples of functional, honest, long-lasting product design. The visual and formal language is the practical embodiment of the Ten Principles for Good Design.

## Key Extractable Lessons

### Form & Hierarchy
- Clear primary controls; secondary functions are present but do not compete
- Geometry is rational and calm; radii and proportions feel inevitable
- Typography on products is restrained, legible, and integrated

### Color & Material
- Limited, purposeful color (often neutrals with precise accent)
- Materials chosen for performance and honesty rather than decoration
- Surfaces are calm; attention goes to the user interaction

### Longevity
- Design that resists fashion
- Repairability and clarity of construction as values
- “As little design as possible” applied rigorously

## Dos Observable
- Make the function self-evident
- Reduce until further reduction would harm usefulness
- Detail every remaining element with care
- Design for years of use, not seasons of novelty

## Caution
- Pure Braun/Rams language can feel cold if applied without attention to human warmth or context
- The principles are more important than copying specific 1960s forms

## When to Reference
- Product design, hardware, and digital interfaces that want timeless functional clarity
- Brand systems that prioritize honesty and longevity over trend

## Related
- Direct source of Rams’s Ten Principles
- Strong influence on Apple product design
- Core reference for Minimalism and functional modernism

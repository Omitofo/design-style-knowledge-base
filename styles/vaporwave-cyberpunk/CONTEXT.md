# Vaporwave & Cyberpunk Aesthetics

## Definition
Two related but distinct internet-born visual languages:

- **Vaporwave**: Nostalgic, surreal, often ironic aesthetic drawing from 1980s–1990s consumer technology, corporate muzak, classical sculpture, Japanese city pop, and early digital graphics. Soft pastels, glitch, Greco-Roman busts, VHS artifacts, and mall-soft ambience.
- **Cyberpunk**: High-tech, low-life future noir — neon, rain-slicked streets, dense urban verticality, corporate dystopia, terminal green/black, glitch, and augmented bodies. Rooted in 1980s science fiction (Gibson, Blade Runner) and later games and film.

Both function as strong mood and subcultural codes rather than complete functional design systems.

## Core Characteristics

### Vaporwave
- Pastel pinks, purples, cyan, soft gradients
- Classical sculpture + early computer graphics + consumer electronics
- Glitch, scan lines, VHS distortion
- Japanese text and 80s/90s commercial imagery
- Dreamy, nostalgic, slightly melancholic or ironic tone

### Cyberpunk
- Neon magenta, cyan, electric blue against deep blacks
- Dense urban nightscapes, rain, reflections
- Terminal typography, glitch, HUD overlays
- High contrast, technological grit
- Dystopian or high-energy futuristic mood

## Dos
- Commit to the cultural references and mood rather than applying surface filters
- Use the aesthetic to support narrative or emotional positioning
- Maintain enough hierarchy and contrast for any required legibility
- Be aware of the subcultural and historical baggage each carries

## Don’ts
- Reduce either to a random neon + glitch preset
- Apply them to serious utility or high-trust contexts without heavy justification
- Ignore readability when text is part of the communication

## Particularities
- Both are highly meme-able and trend-sensitive; they can date quickly or signal very specific cultural alignment.
- Vaporwave often carries ironic or anti-capitalist undertones; cyberpunk carries dystopian or tech-noir ones.
- Best used when the project authentically occupies or comments on those cultural spaces.

## How to Achieve
**Vaporwave**: Pastel palette, classical + retro-tech collage, soft gradients, light glitch, nostalgic type or Japanese commercial lettering.  
**Cyberpunk**: Dark base, neon accents, dense urban or tech imagery, monospaced/terminal type, rain/glitch overlays, high contrast.

## When to Use
- Music, games, fashion, editorial, and cultural projects aligned with the respective moods
- Campaigns that want to signal internet-native or speculative-fiction energy

## When to Avoid
- Corporate, financial, healthcare, or long-lived identity systems
- Contexts that require neutrality or broad accessibility

## Related
- Share experimental and anti-mainstream energy with Punk and Postmodern work
- Can intersect with Maximalism when density is high
- Opposite of Swiss clarity and Luxury Minimal

# Art Nouveau

## Definition
Art Nouveau (c. 1890–1910) is an international style characterized by organic, flowing lines, natural forms (especially plants, flowers, and female figures), and a desire to create a “total work of art” that unified architecture, furniture, graphics, and decorative arts. It rejected academic historicism in favor of new, nature-inspired ornament.

Key figures: Alphonse Mucha, Hector Guimard, Antoni Gaudí, Aubrey Beardsley, Charles Rennie Mackintosh (related Glasgow Style), Victor Horta.

## Core Characteristics
- Whiplash curves and sinuous, organic lines
- Stylized natural motifs (flowers, vines, insects, hair)
- Asymmetry and dynamic composition
- Integration of type and ornament (especially in posters)
- Rich but often harmonious color
- Emphasis on craft and the decorative arts
- Feminine and naturalistic imagery in much of the graphic work

## Dos
- Let line and organic rhythm drive the composition
- Integrate ornament with structure rather than applying it as afterthought
- Use type that can flow with or complement the organic forms
- Maintain overall harmony even when detail is abundant

## Don’ts
- Overcrowd every surface until the design becomes illegible or exhausting
- Apply Art Nouveau motifs as pure pastiche without understanding the linear logic
- Use it for purely utilitarian interfaces where organic ornament fights function

## Particularities
- Art Nouveau was short-lived as a dominant style but extremely influential on later decorative and poster traditions.
- It sits in productive tension with the emerging geometric modernism that followed.
- Excellent for brands or projects that want elegance, nature connection, or fin-de-siècle glamour.

## How to Achieve
1. Begin with strong, flowing linear structure.
2. Develop stylized natural motifs that follow the same rhythmic logic.
3. Integrate typography into the ornamental flow where appropriate.
4. Choose a refined, often muted or jewel-toned palette.
5. Balance density of ornament with areas of rest.

## When to Use
- Beauty, fashion, cultural, hospitality, and heritage projects
- Posters, packaging, and identity work that benefit from organic elegance
- Contexts that want to signal craft, nature, or decorative richness

## When to Avoid
- Strictly modernist, technical, or high-efficiency digital products
- Situations requiring maximum neutrality or geometric clarity

## Related
- Predecessor and sometimes counterpoint to geometric modernism (Bauhaus, Swiss)
- Shares decorative richness with Art Deco but favors organic over geometric forms
- Can inform contemporary maximalist or nature-inspired work

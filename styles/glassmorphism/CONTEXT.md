# Glassmorphism

## Definition
Glassmorphism is a digital visual style characterized by frosted-glass panels, background blur, transparency, subtle borders, and soft depth. Elements appear to float as translucent layers over colorful or detailed backgrounds. It became prominent around 2020–2022 (notably in macOS Big Sur, Windows Fluent, and many web/UI trends).

## Core Characteristics
- Semi-transparent panels with background blur (frosted glass)
- Light borders or subtle outlines to define edges
- Soft shadows for floating elevation
- Vibrant or gradient backgrounds that show through the glass
- Layered depth without heavy skeuomorphism
- Clean, modern, often airy aesthetic

## Dos
- Ensure sufficient contrast between text/icons and the blurred background
- Use blur and transparency purposefully to create hierarchy and focus
- Keep borders and highlights consistent so panels read as a system
- Test readability across different background images and colors
- Consider performance (blur can be expensive)

## Don’ts
- Sacrifice legibility for the glass effect
- Overuse transparency until the interface becomes noisy or confusing
- Apply glassmorphism to every surface without hierarchy
- Ignore accessibility (especially contrast and motion sensitivity)

## Particularities
- Relies heavily on the quality of the background; weak backgrounds make weak glass.
- Works best when there is meaningful content or color behind the panels.
- Can feel trendy and may date more quickly than stricter minimal or system styles.
- Performance and accessibility require extra care compared with opaque flat design.

## How to Achieve
1. Establish a rich or colorful background layer.
2. Create panels with semi-transparent fills + backdrop blur.
3. Add subtle light borders and soft shadows for definition.
4. Ensure text and interactive elements meet contrast requirements against the blurred result.
5. Limit the number of overlapping glass layers to maintain clarity.

## When to Use
- Modern marketing sites, dashboards, and consumer apps that want a premium, contemporary feel
- Interfaces that benefit from layered depth without heavy realism
- Brand experiences that want softness and visual interest

## When to Avoid
- Data-dense or high-productivity tools where clarity and performance are paramount
- Contexts with strict accessibility or low-power device requirements
- Long-lived design systems that need to resist visual trends

## Related
- Evolves from Flat Design and Material by reintroducing soft depth and atmosphere
- Shares floating-panel ideas with certain neumorphic and modern OS aesthetics
- Can be combined carefully with Minimalism or Luxury Minimal

# Punk & Grunge Typography / Design

## Definition
Punk design (mid-1970s onward) and its grunge descendant (especially 1990s) reject professional polish, corporate taste, and modernist order. They favor DIY production values, raw collage, ransom-note or distressed typography, photocopier aesthetics, and an anti-establishment stance. The look signals authenticity through visible imperfection.

Key references: Jamie Reid (Sex Pistols), early punk zines, David Carson (Ray Gun and grunge typography), cut-and-paste aesthetics, 1990s alternative culture.

## Core Characteristics
- Distressed, handwritten, or ransom-style typography
- Collage, overprinting, and layered detritus
- High contrast, often black/white with sudden color bursts
- Visible process: photocopy grain, tape, tears, misregistration
- Asymmetry and deliberate illegibility as attitude
- Anti-grid, anti-hierarchy (or hierarchy achieved through chaos)
- Low-fidelity, “anyone can do this” production values

## Dos
- Make the rawness intentional and consistent with the message
- Use texture and distress to support tone rather than as default decoration
- Maintain enough hierarchy that the core message can still be found
- Match the energy to genuine cultural or brand attitude (not costume)

## Don’ts
- Apply distress filters to otherwise conventional layouts and call it punk
- Sacrifice all legibility when the content needs to be read
- Use the style for brands or contexts that require trust, clarity, or longevity without heavy adaptation
- Confuse “messy” with “designed” — the best punk/grunge work still has strong conceptual force

## Particularities
- Punk is more attitude and production method than a fixed visual system.
- Grunge (Carson et al.) pushed experimental typography further into illegibility and magazine culture.
- Digital tools made “distress” easy; the original power came from actual limitation and urgency.

## How to Achieve
1. Start with content and attitude, not filters.
2. Use real or simulated low-fi processes (scan, photocopy, cut, reassemble).
3. Choose or create type that feels urgent, handmade, or appropriated.
4. Layer elements with visible overlap and texture.
5. Edit so that chaos still has rhythm and points of emphasis.

## When to Use
- Music, youth culture, activist, or anti-corporate projects
- Brands that authentically occupy a rebellious or underground position
- Editorial work that wants to feel raw and immediate

## When to Avoid
- Corporate, financial, healthcare, or luxury contexts that need polish and trust
- Interfaces where usability is the primary goal

## Related
- Shares raw honesty with Brutalism/Neubrutalism
- Overlaps with Postmodern experimental typography
- Opposite of Swiss clarity and Luxury Minimal

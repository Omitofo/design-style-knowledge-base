# Brutalism & Neubrutalism

## Definition
Brutalism originates in post-war architecture (béton brut = raw concrete). In graphic and digital design it means embracing rawness, asymmetry, visible structure, high contrast, and deliberate visual discomfort or honesty. Neubrutalism is the 2010s–2020s web revival: bold type, raw HTML-like aesthetics, thick borders, high-saturation colors, and rejection of polished skeuomorphic or soft UI trends.

## Core Characteristics
- Visible structure and "unfinished" quality
- High contrast (often black/white + one intense accent)
- Thick borders, hard shadows or none at all
- Bold, sometimes deliberately awkward typography
- Asymmetry and uneven spacing used intentionally
- Rejection of soft gradients, subtle shadows, and "pretty" polish
- Honesty about the medium (web, print, concrete)

## Dos
- Make the structure legible and intentional
- Use contrast and scale boldly
- Let the medium show (code, paper, material)
- Pair rawness with clear hierarchy so usability is not sacrificed
- Use it when the brand voice is rebellious, technical, or anti-corporate

## Don’ts
- Confuse "ugly on purpose" with "no design"
- Sacrifice accessibility (contrast, focus states, readable type sizes)
- Apply it to contexts that require trust, calm, or luxury without careful justification
- Overdo randomness — the best brutalist work still has underlying systems

## Particularities
- Architectural brutalism was about honesty of materials and social purpose; digital neubrutalism often mixes that with internet-native irony or punk energy.
- It can feel aggressive; calibrate intensity to audience.
- Strong contrast can aid accessibility if implemented correctly.

## How to Achieve
1. Start with a strong, simple grid then deliberately break or expose it.
2. Choose high-contrast palette (black, white, one loud color).
3. Use bold sans or display type with large size differences.
4. Prefer hard edges, visible borders, minimal or exaggerated shadows.
5. Keep content hierarchy clear even if the surface feels raw.

## When to Use
- Tech products, creative agencies, cultural institutions wanting edge
- Campaigns that need to stand out from polished corporate design
- Portfolios and experimental interfaces

## When to Avoid
- Banking, healthcare, government, or any high-trust utility context without heavy adaptation
- Audiences that prioritize calm and ease

## Related
- Shares honesty with Rams and Swiss (but rejects their restraint)
- Opposite of soft glassmorphism / luxury minimal
- Overlaps with Punk and certain Postmodern approaches
